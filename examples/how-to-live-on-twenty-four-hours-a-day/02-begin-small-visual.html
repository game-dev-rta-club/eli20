The reframe gives personal life, relationships, health, and learning their own central status instead of making them leftovers from employment.

The day within the day

Sixteen hours contain several kinds of ownership.

6–10 p.m.People, body, mind
10 p.m.–6 a.m.Sleep
6–9 a.m.Morning life
9–10 a.m.Journey and transition

The exact blocks vary by person. The idea is psychological ownership: name the non-work hours so they stop feeling empty or accidental.

Energy through variation

A different kind of effort can refresh attention.

Analytical work
→
Walk, conversation, or meal
→
Study or creative work

Bennett argues that mental life often needs a change of occupation, not complete idleness. The practical test is to switch modes and observe what restores usable attention.

Energy and health limits are real and individual. Reframing the day should create agency, not deny fatigue.
